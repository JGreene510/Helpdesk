--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS THIS?
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Helpdesk is a little website that runs on your Raspberry Pi (the tiny
computer). People who need computer help fill in a form on that
website. That creates a "ticket". Your team sees the tickets in a
list, picks them up, fixes the problem, and writes down what they did.

It only works inside your own building or home network. It is not on
the public internet, and it should stay that way (see "Safety rules").


--------------------------------------------------------------------
WORDS YOU WILL SEE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Pi ................ The small computer that runs Helpdesk.
Ticket ............ One request for help, like "My printer is jammed".
Admin ............. A boss account. Can do everything.
Agent ............. A helper account. Can work on tickets, but cannot
                    add people, change email settings, or delete.
Terminal .......... A window where you type commands instead of
                    clicking. On Windows it is called "PowerShell".
Command ........... A line of text you type, then press Enter.
Copy and paste .... You can copy any command from this file and
                    paste it. In PowerShell, right-click to paste.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO READ THE COMMANDS IN THIS GUIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Lines that look like this are things you TYPE (or paste), and then
press the Enter key:

        like this line

Words in CAPITAL LETTERS (PIUSER, PIADDRESS) are blanks. Swap them
for what you wrote in the box at the top. Example: if your login name
is "sam" and the address is "mypi.local", then

        ssh PIUSER@PIADDRESS

becomes

        ssh sam@mypi.local

When you type a password in a terminal, NOTHING appears on the screen
- no dots, no stars. That is normal and it is working. Just type it
and press Enter.

PART 1 - ADD YOUR HELPERS  (skip if you work alone)
====================================================================

Everyone who works on tickets should have their OWN login. Never
share one login between people.

1. Click  "Users"  at the top of the page.
2. Find the box "Add User" and fill it in:

      Username ... letters and numbers, at least 3, no spaces
                   (example: maria)
      Password ... a TEMPORARY password, at least 10 characters
      Email ...... optional
      Role ....... choose one:
                     Agent = can work on tickets. Choose this for
                             most people.
                     Admin = can do everything, including adding
                             people and deleting tickets. Keep
                             this for 1 or 2 trusted people.

3. Click "+ Add User".
4. Tell the person their username and temporary password. The first
   time they log in, Helpdesk makes them choose their own password.

Other things you can do on this screen:
   - The key button ("Password") next to a name sets a new password
     for that person (they must change it when they next log in).
   - The trash can button removes that person.
   - You cannot remove your own account from this screen, so you
     cannot delete yourself by accident.

PART 2 - EVERY DAY: WORKING ON TICKETS
====================================================================

LOG IN
   Open the Helpdesk address, click "Admin Login", type your
   username and password.

SEE NEW TICKETS
   The list does not update by itself. Click the "Refresh" button
   (above the list) to see new tickets. Every row is one ticket:
   its number (like HD-001), the subject, who sent it, what kind of
   problem, how urgent, and its status.

   Status words:
      open ....... nobody has picked it up yet
      assigned ... someone is working on it
      resolved ... finished

FIND A TICKET
   Use the three drop-down boxes (Status, Category, Priority) or
   type in the "Search..." box to narrow the list.

WORK ON A TICKET
   1. Click the  "Open"  button at the end of the row.
   2. A window opens showing the whole request.
   3. Fill in what you need:
         Assign To ......... pick the person doing the work. (If you
                             pick someone, the status changes from
                             open to assigned for you.)
         Category .......... fix it if the person picked the wrong one
         Status ............ open / assigned / resolved
         Resolution Notes .. write what you did, in plain words.
                             The person who asked will be able to
                             read this, so be polite and clear.
   4. Click "Save Changes".

   When a ticket is finished, set Status to "resolved" and write the
   Resolution Notes before you save.

THE "DELETE" BUTTON
   Only Admins see it. It removes the ticket FOREVER. There is no
   undo. In most cases just mark the ticket "resolved" instead.

WHEN YOU ARE DONE
   Click "Logout" at the top. Always do this on a shared computer.

HELPERS GET LOGGED OUT AUTOMATICALLY
   After 1 hour of doing nothing, or 8 hours in total, you are logged
   out. Just log in again. Nothing is lost.


====================================================================
PART 3 - IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
====================================================================

To run commands on the Pi again, open PowerShell and type
        ssh PIUSER@PIADDRESS
then go into the folder:
        cd helpdesk-v4

--------------------------------------------------------------------
A. "I forgot the admin password" or "I missed the first password"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   Log in to the Pi (above), go into the folder, then type:

        node server.js --reset-admin

   It prints a new one-time password. Copy it. Then follow
   Install.txt, Part 8 (log in as Beta with that password, then
   choose your own).

   To reset a different person's login, add their username:

        node server.js --reset-admin maria

--------------------------------------------------------------------
B. "The web page will not open"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   Go through these in order:
   1. Is the Pi plugged in and is its light on? Wait 2 minutes after
      plugging it in.
   2. Is your computer on the same network (same Wi-Fi) as the Pi?
      It will not work from home if the Pi is at the office.
   3. Did you type the address exactly? It must start with  http://
      (not https) and end with  :3000
   4. Log in to the Pi and check Helpdesk is running:

        sudo systemctl status helpdesk

      You want to see the words  active (running)  in green.
      Press the  q  key to get out of that screen.
   5. If it is not running, start it again:

        sudo systemctl restart helpdesk

      Wait 10 seconds and try the web page again.
   6. Still stuck? See the last messages Helpdesk wrote:

        sudo journalctl -u helpdesk -n 50 --no-pager

      Copy that text and send it to whoever helps you with computers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
C. "Too many failed logins. Try again later."
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   Helpdesk locks logins for a while after many wrong passwords. This
   protects you from people guessing. Wait 15 minutes, then try again
   with the right password. (If you do not know the right password,
   use problem A.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
D. "The power went out / the Pi was unplugged"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   Nothing to do. Plug it in, wait 2 minutes, and Helpdesk starts
   by itself. Your tickets are saved on the Pi.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
E. "How do I make a backup?"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   Your tickets and people live in two files. Copy them somewhere
   safe once a week. On the Pi, type these two lines:

        mkdir -p ~/helpdesk-backup
        cd ~/helpdesk-v4
        cp tickets.json users.json ~/helpdesk-backup/

   That makes a folder called helpdesk-backup with the copies. Best
   of all, also copy that folder to a USB stick or another computer.
   A backup that lives only on the Pi is lost if the Pi breaks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
F. "How do I stop or start Helpdesk on purpose?"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   Stop:     sudo systemctl stop helpdesk
   Start:    sudo systemctl start helpdesk
   Restart:  sudo systemctl restart helpdesk

--------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------

PART 4 - OPTIONAL: EMAILS WHEN TICKETS ARRIVE
====================================================================

Skip this on day one. Helpdesk works fine without email. Do it later
if you want these two things:
   - You get an email when a new ticket arrives.
   - The person who asked gets an email when you change their
     ticket (for example when it is resolved).

You need a Gmail account that Helpdesk can send from. Do NOT use the
normal Gmail password - Google gives you a special one for this.

1. Sign in to that Gmail account in your browser.
2. Turn on "2-Step Verification" for it. (Google requires this.)
   Search the web for:  google account 2-step verification
3. Go to   https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords
   Create a new app password. Name it  Helpdesk.
   Google shows 16 letters. Copy them.
4. In Helpdesk, log in as an Admin, click "Users", then click the
   "Email" tab.
5. Fill in:
      Gmail App Password ........... paste the 16 letters
      From Email ................... that Gmail address
      Admin Notification Email ..... where YOU want new-ticket emails
   Tick the boxes:
      [x] Notify on new ticket
      [x] Notify on status change
      [x] Email the assignee when a ticket is assigned to them
      [x] Enable email notifications
6. Click "Save Settings". Then click "Send Test". Check your inbox.

If the test fails, the red message now tells you why (for example,
"Gmail refused the login - use an App Password"). Fix that and try
again.

When you assign a ticket to a person, that person gets an email too -
but only if their user account has an email address. For people you
already added: Users -> click "Email" next to their name and type
it in. You will not get an email for a ticket you assign to
yourself.

After you click Save on a ticket, the green message tells you what
happened with the email, for example "Email queued to Sam" or "No email
was sent to Sam: they have no email address saved". If you ever think
an email is missing, read that message first.

You can move a ticket back to Open at any time (for example from
Resolved). Helpdesk keeps the choice you make.

When a ticket is marked Resolved, both the person who submitted it
and the technician it was assigned to get an email.

Emails are sent in the background, so a ticket never waits on them.
If Gmail is slow or fails, Helpdesk tries again after 30 seconds,
2 minutes and 10 minutes. Under the Email settings, "Recent email
activity" shows what was SENT, is RETRYING, FAILED or was DROPPED.

Helpdesk limits how many emails it sends per hour (10 per person,
60 in total), on purpose, so it cannot be used to flood anyone's
inbox. Anything dropped for that reason shows up in the activity list.


====================================================================
SAFETY RULES  (please read these; they are short)
====================================================================

1. KEEP IT INSIDE YOUR NETWORK.
   Do not set up "port forwarding" on your router for Helpdesk, and do
   not put its address on the internet. It does not use the secure
   (https) kind of connection. Inside your own network that is okay.
   If people need it from outside, ask an IT person to set up a VPN.

2. ONE LOGIN PER PERSON. Never share passwords. When someone leaves,
   remove them (Users -> trash can).

3. NEVER TYPE PASSWORDS INTO TICKETS. Tell your team the same.

4. USE STRONG PASSWORDS. 10+ characters; random words work well.

5. BACK UP WEEKLY (Part 3, problem E).

6. KEEP THE PI UP TO DATE ONCE A MONTH. Log in to the Pi and type
   these two lines, one at a time:

        sudo apt update
        sudo apt upgrade -y

   Then restart the Pi:   sudo reboot
   (Wait 2 minutes. Helpdesk comes back by itself.)

7. THINK TWICE BEFORE TURNING THE PASSWORD OFF.
   It lets EVERYONE on your network (guests on your Wi-Fi too) read
   every ticket. Only do it on a network you fully trust. Boss jobs
   (adding people, email settings, deleting tickets) still need the
   admin password either way. See Install.txt, Part 11.
